W65C816 / RP2354B Computer
Revision-A Board Design Contract
================================

1. Purpose
----------

Revision A is a 5 V, physical W65C816S computer with a 3.3 V RP2354B
daughterboard. The W65C816 executes the firmware, OS, applications, game
logic, and interrupt handlers. The RP2354B is a memory-mapped multimedia and
I/O chipset; it provides video, audio, storage, USB host support, input,
PSRAM, DMA/blitter services, and the bridge to an ESP32-C3 networking
processor.

The ESP32-C3 is the sole W5500 SPI master. The CPU does not access the ESP32-C3
or W5500 directly.

The architecture must remain recognisably a W65C816 computer, not an RP2354B
computer with a legacy CPU attached.


2. Revision-A targets
---------------------

| Area | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| W65C816 supply and bus | 5 V |
| SRAM, Flash, mainboard glue | 5 V |
| RP2354B, ESP32-C3, QMI PSRAM | 3.3 V |
| CPU clocks | 1 MHz bring-up, 4 MHz validation, 8 MHz supported |
| Later clocks | 10/12/14 MHz experimental after proof |
| 20 MHz | Future optimized revision only |
| Standard SRAM | 4 x AS6C4008-55PIN = 2 MiB |
| Optional SRAM | 4 further AS6C4008 footprints = 4 MiB maximum |
| Flash | SST39SF040-55-4I-NHE, PLCC-32 socket |
| Critical glue | 5 V 74AHC, preferably DIP/socketed |
| RP data boundary | SN74LXC8T245, A/VCCA=5 V system bus and B/VCCB=3.3 V RP bus |

Use turned-pin sockets for the CPU, SRAM, Flash socket, and important DIP
logic. Keep the SRAM, CPU, latch, and critical buffers physically close.


3. Power domains and startup
----------------------------

One 5 V system input powers the W65C816 mainboard, memory, Flash, 5 V AHC
logic, and USB VBUS. A hardware-enabled 3.3 V regulator powers the RP2354B,
QMI PSRAM, ESP32-C3, SD interface, and 3.3 V daughterboard logic.

The daughterboard must not require a separate source. The regulator must start
from system 5 V without relying on firmware. Every IC has a local 100 nF
decoupling capacitor.

Power-up and brownout must not backfeed 5 V or 3.3 V through GPIO, translators,
USB, SD, ESD devices, or protection diodes. Test cold startup, warm reset,
rapid cycling, 5 V brownout, 3.3 V brownout, reset-held RP, absent daughterboard,
and a fitted daughterboard with no firmware.


4. Mechanical interface
-----------------------

The boards use the existing 98-pin ISA-form-factor card-edge connector and
right-angle adapter. It is selected for robust insertion, keyed orientation,
contact count, availability, and secure daughterboard mounting. It is not an
ISA electrical interface.

The pinout is project-specific and provisional until PCB closure. Reserve,
ground, or explicitly mark every unused connector pin. Review every pinout
change because it can affect signal assignment, voltage domains, timing,
firmware, and routing.


5. Mainboard CPU and bank latch
--------------------------------

The CPU is W65C816S6PG-14 in DIP-40. It multiplexes A16-A23 onto D0-D7 during
PHI2 low.

U2 is a 74AHC573 transparent latch. Its enable is PHI2_N generated directly by
one 74AHC04 gate from PHI2. U2 is transparent during PHI2 low, allowing the
bank address to propagate early in the cycle, then closes around PHI2 rising.

The timing proof must demonstrate:

    CPU minimum bank-address hold time
      > inverter maximum delay + latch closing requirement + PCB skew + margin

Use maximum logic delay, supply and temperature bounds, estimated loading,
package delay, trace skew, and edge degradation. Place the inverter by U2 and
U2 by the CPU. The latched bank outputs remain stable for the complete memory
cycle.


6. Memory system
----------------

Fit U20-U23 with AS6C4008-55PIN (512 KiB x 8 each) for the 2 MiB standard
configuration. Provide U24-U27 as the same optional footprint for 4 MiB
maximum. Each fitted SRAM receives its own active-low chip select.

The target is zero-wait SRAM at 8 MHz. It is accepted only when every
worst-case path has at least 10 ns calculated margin, including CPU address
validity, bank-latch delay, address buffering, select decode, SRAM access,
U3 delay, CPU setup/hold, duty cycle, loading, temperature, supply, and PCB
skew.

Address loading is a design item, not an assumption. Provide optional 74AHC244
buffer footprints and zero-ohm bypass options for A0-A7 and A8-A15. Evaluate
additional buffering for Flash, bank-address fanout, long routes, and the
connector. Balance CPU load and edge quality against added propagation delay.

U3 remains the memory-side 74AHC245. It isolates the CPU during the bank phase,
reduces bus loading, controls memory access, and offers a measurement boundary.
It is enabled only on valid selected memory cycles during PHI2 high; it is
disabled during PHI2 low, reset, RP cycles, invalid cycles, and future DMA
ownership. Its direction follows RWB.


7. Flash, strobes, and wait states
----------------------------------

U30 is SST39SF040-55-4I-NHE in a PLCC-32 socket. It must hold the vectors,
boot monitor, diagnostics, and firmware image. Verify exact PLCC pinout and
programming command compatibility before changing its schematic symbol.

Flash programming is hardware-protected. Programming enable defaults inactive,
regardless of firmware state. Conceptually:

    FLASH_WE_N = NOT(FLASH_SELECTED AND FLASH_PROGRAM_ENABLE AND NOT(RWB) AND PHI2)

Memory controls are PHI2-qualified:

    RAM_OE_N   = NOT(RAM_SELECTED   AND RWB          AND PHI2)
    RAM_WE_N   = NOT(RAM_SELECTED   AND NOT(RWB)     AND PHI2)
    FLASH_OE_N = NOT(FLASH_SELECTED AND RWB          AND PHI2)

No write pulse may occur while address/data changes, and decode glitches must
not produce writes. Place and terminate unused CMOS gates and inputs.

Revision A has independent mainboard wait-state logic. It must decode a target
while PHI2 is low, latch the requirement, assert/release RDY synchronously,
avoid glitches, and ensure one side effect per stretched access.

| Target | Initial policy |
|--------|----------------|
| SRAM | 0 waits |
| Flash | 1 wait; optional zero-wait strap after proof |
| RP registers/FIFOs | At least 1 fixed wait; more if required |
| Slow expansion | Configurable |

All RDY sources are open-drain/open-collector. No source may drive RDY high.
Wait logic must work from reset and must not depend on RP firmware.


8. RP memory-mapped interface
-----------------------------

Revision A connects A0-A9, RP_CS_N, PHI2, RWB, VDA, VPA, RESET_N, RDY, and
D0-D7 to the RP interface. A10-A23 may remain reserved raw-bus connector
signals for later debug or deterministic hardware; they are not direct
Revision-A RP inputs.

| Range | Function |
|-------|----------|
| $F00000-$F000FF | RP control/status registers |
| $F00100-$F001FF | Command FIFO write window |
| $F00200-$F002FF | Response FIFO read window |
| $F00300-$F003FF | Input/event FIFO read window |

The existing decode is `$F00000-$F1FFFF`. Because A10-A16 are not decoded, the
1 KiB interface is intentionally mirrored 128 times. A8-A9 select the four
pages; FIFO page addresses may alias one FIFO port to support sequential CPU
transfers. There is no direct shared-memory PSRAM window in Revision A.

The PIO bus engine qualifies RP_CS_N, PHI2, RWB, VDA, VPA, reset, and wait
state. It must distinguish cycle-detected, cycle-waiting, and cycle-completed
states. Register writes, FIFO transfers, acknowledgements, and DMA submission
are all one-shot operations even when an access is stretched.


9. RP 3.3 V / mainboard 5 V boundary
-------------------------------------

U16 is SN74LXC8T245. Connect its A side/VCCA to the 5 V system data bus and its
B side/VCCB to the RP 3.3 V data bus. The device convention is DIR high for
A-to-B, so set `DIR = NOT(RWB)`: CPU write (`RWB=0`) is system-to-RP and CPU
read (`RWB=1`) is RP-to-system. `/OE` and `DIR` are therefore 5 V-domain
controls referenced to VCCA. `/OE` has an external 5 V pull-up and defaults
disabled.

U16 can enable only when all of the following are true:

    MAIN_5V_VALID AND RP_3V3_VALID AND RP_RESET_RELEASED
    AND RP_SELECTED AND PHI2 AND (VDA OR VPA)

It is disabled during reset, PHI2 low, non-RP cycles, invalid cycles, absent
or unpowered daughterboard, and before RP firmware/bus readiness. The RP data
GPIO are inputs/high impedance except while driving a selected CPU read.

Every 5 V connector input must first pass through a dedicated power-off-safe 3.3 V
input buffer at the daughterboard boundary. Direct 5 V on an RP GPIO is not the
normal level-shifting method. An individually verified RP2354B digital FT pin may
be used directly only as a documented input-only fault-resilience fallback, with
verified startup/brownout behaviour and no unpowered 3.3 V domain; it must never
be used as a 5 V output.

IOVDD is operating at 3.3 V !!!!!

Non-FT pins must not receive 5 V. FT pins should receive 3.3 V by design; 5 V
tolerance is only fault resilience. !!!

Never use analog-capable/non-FT GPIO for direct 5 V. Small 220 ohm to 1 kohm
series resistors may provide damping and limited injection-current protection;
they are not voltage dividers. PHI2 must not use a resistor-divider translator.
The required input buffers are the normal path for all RP connector inputs.

RDY and RP_IRQ_N are 5 V mainboard nets with mainboard pull-ups. The RP uses
fast open-drain/open-collector stages that default released when the board is
absent, unpowered, reset, brownout-reset, or unstarted. Their propagation and
pull-up rise time are part of the timing calculation.


10. Interrupt, NMI, PSRAM, and DMA rules
----------------------------------------

The RP exports one active-low open-drain `RP_IRQ_N`. It has pending, enable,
acknowledge (write-one-to-clear), and vector registers. Initial sources include
VBlank/raster, audio, input, USB, storage, network, DMA completion/error, and
RP faults. IRQ remains asserted while any enabled source remains pending.

NMI is exclusively physical RESTORE/debug/emergency functionality. It must not
carry ordinary video, audio, storage, input, network, USB, or DMA events.

RP QMI PSRAM stores framebuffers, tiles, sprites, fonts, audio, file/network
buffers, decompression space, and DMA/blitter staging. The CPU accesses it via
indirect registers, FIFOs, auto-increment, DMA, and block commands; no CPU code
executes from PSRAM in Revision A.

Long operations are asynchronous. CPU register/FIFO accesses may wait briefly,
but SD, network, blits, files, and large transfers must return completion by
status and IRQ. Empty FIFO reads must return a defined condition or use a bounded
timeout, never hold RDY indefinitely.


11. ESP32-C3, Ethernet, SD, USB, video, and audio
--------------------------------------------------

The RP and ESP use full-duplex UART with hardware flow control:

| Function | RP2354B | ESP32-C3 |
|----------|---------|----------|
| RP TX / ESP RX | GPIO36 | GPIO20 |
| RP RX / ESP TX | GPIO37 | GPIO21 |
| ESP RTS_N / RP CTS_N | GPIO38 | GPIO0 |
| RP RTS_N / ESP CTS_N | GPIO39 | GPIO1 |
| ESP enable | GPIO10 | EN |
| ESP boot control | GPIO11 | GPIO9 |

ESP GPIO9 has an explicit safe pull-up. GPIO18/GPIO19 remain for USB
Serial/JTAG. The protocol includes type, length, sequence, payload, and CRC,
plus bounded queues, timeouts/retries, error counters, capability discovery,
events, and reset recovery.

The W5500 has complete SPI, 25 MHz clock, reset, interrupt, chip-select bias,
LED, decoupling, magnetics/RJ45, and shield-grounding treatment. Its interrupt
terminates at the ESP, not the CPU/RP IRQ net.

Use 4-bit SDIO with CLK, CMD, DAT0-DAT3, card detect, pull-ups, suitable series
damping, ESD protection, and verified connector footprint. USB uses an RP-host
USB hub with VBUS switch, ganged enable/overcurrent feedback, ESD protection,
and manual differential-pair routing. USB VBUS stays at 5 V without rail
backfeed.

Video uses RP HSTX. Freeze either DVI-compatible output or a genuinely complete
HDMI implementation including HPD/DDC/CEC; do not claim HDMI if only DVI
signalling is fitted. Route video pairs manually. Audio uses RP PSRAM-backed
multi-channel sample playback, DMA/block buffering, FIFO thresholds, and
completion interrupts so it does not need cycle-perfect CPU service.


12. Implementation and validation sequence
------------------------------------------

1. Reconcile documentation and voltage domains.
2. Convert mainboard power/critical glue to 5 V AHC and verify decoupling.
3. Implement U2/U18 PHI2 inversion and complete bank-latch calculation.
4. Confirm standard/optional SRAM footprints, optional AHC244/bypass paths,
   decode loading, Flash, and hardware write protection.
5. Qualify U3 and all memory strobes; terminate unused gates.
6. Add independent wait-state logic and test points.
7. Verify the RP FT/input strategy, U16 boundary, power sequencing, RDY, and
   IRQ stages.
8. Complete RP core/PSRAM, then ESP/W5500, then SD/USB/video/audio.
9. Assign and verify every footprint against the selected manufacturer part.
10. Resolve unexplained ERC issues, freeze pinout/voltage/timing tables, then
    proceed to PCB changes.

Run ERC after each schematic subsystem. Run DRC after PCB changes. Do not
autoroute USB or video differential pairs. Do not generate manufacturing files
until explicitly requested.

Bring-up runs mainboard-only at 1, 4, then 8 MHz; Flash wait-state; passive
daughterboard; minimal RP registers; FIFO; interrupt; PSRAM/DMA; ESP; storage,
USB, video, and audio. Only then may 10/12/14 MHz be tested. Each accepted
frequency requires formal calculation, logic-analyser captures, memory stress,
RP/FIFO/interrupt tests, and supply/temperature consideration.


13. Required measurements and acceptance
-----------------------------------------

Provide test points for PHI2, PHI2_N, RESET_N, RWB, VDA, VPA, RDY, IRQ_N,
RP_CS_N, U2 enable, RAM/Flash selects and strobes, U3/U16 enable/direction,
selected data and bank lines, 5 V, and 3.3 V.

Calculate CPU-bank-to-latch, CPU-address-to-SRAM, bank-to-decode-to-memory,
SRAM/Flash-to-U3-to-CPU, write, strobe, bus-release, RP-wait, U16 read/write,
RDY/IRQ, and translator power sequencing paths. Use maximum propagation delay,
minimum setup/hold, duty-cycle bounds, supply/temperature, loading, package,
and PCB skew. Zero-wait paths need 10 ns target margin.

Revision A is accepted only when the mainboard runs at 5 V; standard 2 MiB
SRAM is zero-wait at 8 MHz with the calculated margin; Flash boots with one
wait; the mainboard safely boots without the daughterboard; no RP non-FT pin
receives 5 V; no rail backfeeds; U3/U16 cannot contend; memory writes are
PHI2-qualified; stretched cycles cause one side effect; RP transfers and IRQ
work at 8 MHz; all normal events avoid NMI; ERC/DRC have no unexplained issues;
and analyser captures confirm the timing table.

Future work may explore CPLD decode/waits, full address observation, tighter
decode, shared-memory windows, faster/SMD RAM, programmable clocks, guaranteed
14 MHz, 20 MHz optimization, cache/prefetch, and more DMA features. These must
not compromise the rule that the W65C816 is the computer and the RP2354B is its
chipset.
